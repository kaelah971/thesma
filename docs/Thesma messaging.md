# THESMA — MESSAGING

**Master brand:** Thesma  
**Category:** Autonomous market for verified business outcomes  
**Primary line:** Set the outcome. Let the agent find the price.  
**Supporting line:** Pay for what happens, not what was promised.  
**Status:** Product-language system for the target product; live capability claims require evidence.

> This document converts the non-visual parts of the Thesma scope into product language, UX copy, narrative and message behaviour. Visual identity guidance is intentionally excluded.

## Status vocabulary

Use these labels whenever a sentence could otherwise imply more than the product proves:

- **Shipped** — implemented and exercised in the current build.
- **Target** — specified for the MVP or current build.
- **Roadmap** — intentionally deferred.
- **Hypothesis** — requires user, market or performance validation.

Do not describe target wallet flows, live Arc settlement, automatic payouts, customer traction, Partner adoption or Circle support as shipped without implementation evidence.

# 1. Product identity

## 1.1 The plain description

> **Thesma is an autonomous market where businesses and Partners trade verified customer outcomes.**

## 1.2 The fuller description

> A company sets a customer goal, budget and hard limits. Thesma’s Demand Agent adjusts the price of the next verified result. Partner Supply Agents decide which opportunities are worth their attention. A verifier confirms the purchase before the Partner is paid.

## 1.3 The category sentence

> **The autonomous market for verified outcomes.**

Use the category sentence wherever a new audience needs to understand what Thesma is before hearing why it is different.

## 1.4 The central idea

> **Set the outcome. Let the agent find the price.**

This is the organising sentence for the homepage, demo, pitch and product onboarding.

## 1.5 The customer relief

Before Thesma:

- A company pays for activity and hopes it becomes a result.
- The commission stays fixed even when the market changes.
- Growth decisions sit in spreadsheets, chats and manual approvals.
- Partners waste attention on campaigns that do not fit.
- Attribution and payout evidence are reconciled after the fact.

After Thesma:

- The outcome is defined before the market opens.
- Price responds to pace, supply, budget and performance.
- Partners can decide inside their own rules.
- Payment follows verified evidence.
- The company can explain what the agent did and why.

## 1.6 The technical mechanism

Introduce the mechanism after the outcome is understood:

> Demand and Supply Agents operate inside human-set limits. A deterministic verifier establishes whether the result happened. Policy and contract controls enforce authority. Arc settles USDC when the supported implementation is live.

Do not lead with:

> AI-powered, blockchain-native, agentic growth infrastructure.

That describes machinery before meaning.

# 2. Messaging house

## Roof: the one idea to remember

> **Thesma turns a business goal into a live market for verified results.**

## Pillar 1 — Buy the result

### Message

Thesma pays for the qualifying result, not for activity that merely surrounds it.

### Why it matters

Businesses need measurable customer acquisition, not a pile of clicks, posts and impressions.

### Proof path

- The first MVP outcome is a qualifying paid customer.
- Clicks and signups remain visible but do not earn the bounty alone.
- A qualifying purchase must pass attribution, payment and uniqueness checks.
- Customer revenue remains on the company side of the money flow.

### Copy examples

- “A click can start the journey. Only a verified purchase earns the bounty.”
- “Your campaign pays when the agreed result happens.”
- “Activity is a signal. The outcome is the settlement.”

## Pillar 2 — Let the price move

### Message

The next result should not cost the same in every market condition.

### Why it matters

A static commission cannot respond to a deadline, thin supply, weak conversion or budget pressure.

### Proof path

- Public bounty changes inside floor and ceiling.
- Partner-specific terms can reflect fit and performance.
- The Demand Agent can raise, lower, hold, pause or reallocate within policy.
- A deterministic pricing kernel creates the allowed range.

### Copy examples

- “The price moves when the market moves.”
- “Raise the signal when the campaign falls behind pace.”
- “Spend pressure changes the decision, not the rules.”

## Pillar 3 — Give agents room, not the keys

### Message

Thesma gives the agent space to act without giving it unrestricted treasury authority.

### Why it matters

A useful agent should not ask for permission on every small decision. A safe agent should not be able to rewrite the mandate.

### Proof path

- Owner defines goal, budget, floor, ceiling, qualification and deadline.
- Campaign budget is separated from customer revenue.
- Hard policy fields are not agent-editable.
- Contract and policy checks run again before execution.

### Copy examples

- “Set the boundary once. Let the agent work inside it.”
- “Autonomy belongs inside a budget you can name.”
- “The agent can change the price. It cannot change the ceiling.”

## Pillar 4 — Make every decision inspectable

### Message

Autonomous action should leave a clear record of what happened and why.

### Why it matters

A polished explanation is not enough when money moves. People need evidence, state and a path to review.

### Proof path

- Every reprice, pause, counter and payout creates a decision record.
- Outcome receipts link attribution and payment evidence.
- Pending, verified, submitted, settled, rejected and returned are separate states.
- Ambiguous chain state triggers reconciliation rather than a blind retry.

### Copy examples

- “Every payout has a reason.”
- “See what the agent saw before it acted.”
- “The record remains after the decision.”

# 3. Message hierarchy

## 3.1 Five-second message

> Thesma is an autonomous market for verified customer outcomes.

## 3.2 Homepage hero

### Recommended

**Set the outcome. Let the agent find the price.**

Set a customer goal and a hard budget. Thesma’s agents find, price and settle the next verified result.

**Primary CTA:** See how the market works  
**Secondary CTA:** Start a campaign

### Alternative hero for Partners

**Put your attention where the price is worth it.**

Your Supply Agent watches live opportunities, respects your rules and negotiates when the market is worth joining.

**Primary CTA:** Browse live markets  
**Secondary CTA:** Set Partner rules

## 3.3 Supporting headline options

- Pay for what happens, not what was promised.
- A live market for measurable growth.
- The next customer has a price. Let the market find it.
- Agents can act. Your rules still decide how far.
- Where demand meets attention at a verified price.

## 3.4 Short pitch

> Thesma lets businesses set a customer goal and bounded budget, then gives a Demand Agent room to adjust the price as the market changes. Partner Agents choose opportunities that fit their rules. When a qualifying purchase is verified, the right Partner is paid.

## 3.5 Thirty-second pitch

> Affiliate programs usually fix the commission and leave the economics manual. Thesma turns acquisition into a live two-sided market. The company sets the target, budget, maximum CAC and qualifying purchase. Partners join, skip or negotiate through their own agents. Thesma changes the price inside those limits and pays only after attribution and payment evidence verify the outcome. Company revenue and campaign budget stay separate.

## 3.6 Investor or judge pitch

> Thesma is a two-sided autonomous outcome market. A company’s Demand Agent continuously prices verified customer acquisition while Partner Supply Agents decide when the price justifies deploying attention. The LLM does not invent prices or control unrestricted funds: a deterministic pricing kernel produces a safe range, policy and contract controls enforce authority, and a verifier establishes whether a qualifying purchase happened. The MVP proves this loop with a bounded campaign, Arc USDC payment, dynamic repricing and automatic payout.

## 3.7 Full narrative

Businesses can automate activity, but they still manage the economics by hand. Commissions stay fixed even when the market changes. Partners see more campaigns than they can evaluate. Customers generate revenue, but attribution and payout evidence are stitched together later.

Thesma changes the unit of exchange. The company does not post a task and hope for effort. It sets a measurable outcome and a budget. The Demand Agent operates inside those limits, while each Partner’s Supply Agent decides whether the current price is worth the attention required. The market responds to pace, supply, conversion and budget. A verifier confirms the purchase. Only then does the agreed bounty settle.

The result is not “AI that runs marketing.” It is a market where agents can make useful economic decisions without making authority invisible.

# 5. Product movement and UX copy

## 5.1 The user movement

> **Set → Review → Fund → Open → Match → Verify → Pay → Learn**

### Set

The owner states the desired outcome in ordinary language.

> What result do you want to buy?

### Review

Thesma converts intent into a policy.

> I turned your request into a campaign policy. Review the limits before funding it.

### Fund

The owner makes only the campaign budget available.

> The market is not live yet. Fund the campaign to make its budget spendable.

### Open

The market becomes available to eligible Partners.

> Your market is open. The public bounty is 2.00 USDC per verified customer.

### Match

Partner Agents decide whether the opportunity fits.

> Your agent found a possible fit. It will not join unless the campaign and price match your rules.

### Verify

The system establishes whether a qualifying result happened.

> Payment received. Checking attribution, amount, timing and duplicate status.

### Pay

The current terms are applied after verification.

> Outcome verified. Preparing the Partner payout at the agreed price.

### Learn

The decision and result are recorded for the next market decision.

> Alice is converting above the current market average. The agent proposed a bounded price increase.

# 11. Agent language rules

## 11.1 Interpretation versus authorization

Use:

- “I interpreted your request as…"
- “I prepared a policy…"
- “You approved the campaign…"
- “The action passed the campaign policy…"
- “The payout was submitted…"
- “The payout settled…"

Do not use:

- “I understood everything automatically.”
- “I guaranteed the result.”
- “I paid successfully” when the chain only acknowledged submission.
- “The campaign completed” when pending outcomes remain.

## 11.2 The six action states

### Recommend

> I recommend raising the public bounty. Nothing has changed yet.

### Prepare

> I prepared the policy for your review. No campaign is live yet.

### Execute

> The price change passed the policy checks and was submitted.

### Hold

> I am holding this payout because the required evidence is incomplete.

### Refuse

> I cannot execute this action because it exceeds the approved ceiling.

### Escalate

> This requires owner approval because it changes a hard campaign limit.

## 11.3 Explanation structure

Every material action should answer:

1. What changed?
2. Why now?
3. What evidence supported it?
4. What limit constrained it?
5. Did money move?
6. What can the user do next?

Example:

> Raised the public bounty from 2.00 to 2.25 USDC because the campaign is behind pace. The projected CAC remains below the 4.00 ceiling. No customer revenue moved; this action changes the next available Partner price only.

## Cadence rules

- Put the amount, state and reason close together.
- Use concrete verbs.
- Prefer one action per sentence when money is involved.

## Language to avoid

# 13. Objection responses

## “Is this just affiliate marketing?”

> No. Affiliate systems usually keep the commission fixed. Thesma turns the campaign into a live market where price, allocation and participation can change inside policy as pace, supply and performance change.

## “Why not just use a creator campaign platform?”

> Campaign platforms help coordinate people and deliverables. Thesma is focused on the economic decision: what is the next verified result worth right now, and should the agent change the price?

## “Can the agent spend all our money?”

> The target design prevents that. The agent receives a campaign-scoped budget and owner-defined limits. It cannot rewrite hard policy or withdraw general treasury funds.

## “Can a Partner game the system?”

> The target verifier checks attribution, payment evidence, uniqueness, amount, timing and refund rules. Self-referral and replay controls are also required. No fraud claim should be considered complete until these controls are implemented and tested.

## “Does a higher price guarantee more customers?”

> No. A higher price changes the market incentive. It does not guarantee a result.

## “What does Arc add?”

> Arc is the target settlement environment for the campaign and Partner payout flow. The customer promise is still the verified outcome; Arc is part of the evidence and settlement mechanism.

## “Is this live?”

> The product definition and build plan are ready. Live wallet, payout, traction and provider claims depend on the current implementation and must be labelled accordingly.

# 18. Messaging tests

## Five-second comprehension test

Show only the hero and ask:

> What is Thesma, and what does it pay for?

Pass condition: the person can say “a market for verified customer results” without reading the technical section.

## Difference test

Ask:

> How is this different from an affiliate platform?

Pass condition: the person mentions moving price, two-sided agents or verified outcomes.

## Authority test

Ask:

> What can the agent do, and what can it not do?

Pass condition: the person can identify campaign budget, ceilings and owner-controlled policy.

## Evidence test

Ask:

> What must happen before a Partner is paid?

Pass condition: the person mentions a qualifying, attributed and verified purchase.

## Repeatability test

Ask the person to describe Thesma to someone else after a short demo.

Pass condition: they preserve the words `goal`, `price`, `verified result` and `rules`.

## Message experiments

Test one variable at a time:

- “Set the outcome” versus “Pay for the result.”
- “Live market” versus “dynamic campaign.”
- “Partner” versus “creator” for the supply persona.
- “Verified customer” versus “qualified outcome” for the MVP result.
- “Agent finds the price” versus “Agent manages the campaign.”

Do not test inflated claims against honest claims and choose the louder one without checking comprehension and trust.
